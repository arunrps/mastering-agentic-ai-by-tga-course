# Shared-Backend Degradation Affecting Two Endpoints

Incident date 2026-05-21. Severity 2. Duration 71 minutes from first alert to recovery. Owning
team fulfilment-core. Times below are given as offsets from the first alert rather than as clock
times, because the reconstruction was assembled after the fact and the offsets are what the
evidence actually supports.

## Summary

Two endpoints served by inventory-service, /cart and /orders, degraded within a few minutes of
each other. Latency rose first on both and errors followed. No deployment had occurred to
inventory-service or to any adjacent service within the preceding twelve hours, and none was
rolled back. The condition was traced to a large population of cached inventory entries expiring
simultaneously, having been written together during a routine bulk refresh several days earlier
and having been given identical lifetimes at that time. Recovery followed repopulation of the
affected state by fulfilment-core.

The investigation reached a wrong conclusion first and held it for roughly half the incident. That
part is recorded below in more detail than the resolution, because it is the more useful part.

## Impact

Baskets displayed slowly and then, for a growing minority of attempts, not at all. Order
submission failed for a proportion of attempts across the same period. Affected request counts
placed the incident at Sev 2 under POL-SEV-001. No divergence between cart and order views was
identified during or after the incident, which is worth stating explicitly because it was assumed
during the incident and the assumption shaped the first hour.

## Timeline

**T+0.** Alert on /orders, error rate above objective and clearing the evidence gate. /cart was at
that point elevated on latency only and had not yet produced an error signal that cleared the
floor.

**T+4.** Responder confirmed both endpoints elevated. Corroboration noted: two endpoints on one
backend degrading in the same window points at the backend rather than at either endpoint's own
path.

**T+9.** Capacity checked. Request volume across both endpoints was flat, and marginally below the
same hour on the preceding two comparable days. The capacity hypothesis was correctly discarded at
this point, on evidence, in under ten minutes. This part of the response worked.

**T+12.** Change correlation attempted. No deployment to inventory-service in the preceding twelve
hours. No rollback was performed at any point in this incident and POL-DEPLOY-001 was not engaged.

**T+15 to T+38.** The investigation pursued the write path. See below.

**T+38.** Onset ordering examined. /cart latency was found to have stepped several minutes before
/orders moved at all.

**T+44.** Cache hit rate for /cart found to have fallen sharply in the same minute as its latency
step. Escalated to fulfilment-core with that correlation attached.

**T+52.** Fulfilment-core identified the expiry pattern and began repopulating.

**T+71.** Both endpoints within objective. Incident closed at T+95 after a hold period.

## Detection and the evidence gate

Worth recording separately, because it nearly cost more time than the wrong hypothesis did.

At T+0 only /orders produced a verdict that cleared the evidence gate. /cart was elevated on
latency but its error count had not yet reached the failure minimum, so its error series returned
EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT. The first written summary of the incident recorded /orders as elevated and
/cart as not elevated.

That summary was wrong in a way POL-SLO-001 anticipates and explicitly warns against. EVIDENCE
INSUFFICIENT is not a finding of health; it is a statement that the observation available did not
clear the bar. /cart was at that moment materially degraded on latency, which the same verdict
recorded plainly, and the error series simply had not caught up yet. Compressing the two series
into a single word and choosing the reassuring one removed the endpoint from consideration for
several minutes and contributed directly to the write-path hypothesis taking hold: if /cart was
not elevated, /orders was the only endpoint in scope, and the corroboration noted at T+4 read as
weaker than it was.

The corrected practice is the one the policy already states. Report the outcome name rather than a
paraphrase of it, and report the two series separately where they disagree. Neither of those is a
new rule and both were available at the time.

## The hypothesis that was wrong

Between T+15 and T+38 the working hypothesis was that the write path was primarily at fault and
that /cart was degraded as a consequence. Effort during that period went into retry safety, into
whether failed order submissions had left state behind, and into whether a divergence was
developing. None of it was wasted in the sense of being careless — the checks were the right checks
for that hypothesis and they were run correctly — but the hypothesis was wrong and the twenty-three
minutes did not advance the incident.

Two things produced it. The first is that /orders failures were louder. A customer who cannot
complete an order says so immediately and through channels that reach an incident quickly, whereas
a slow basket produces complaints that are vaguer and arrive later. The volume of signal from
/orders was much higher than from /cart and it drew attention proportionally. The second is that
/orders is the endpoint the team considers dangerous, for good reasons documented in RB-INV-002,
and an endpoint that is treated as dangerous attracts the assumption that it is where the problem
is.

Neither of those is evidence. Both felt like evidence at the time.

## The evidence that displaced it

At T+38 a responder compared the onset times of the two endpoints rather than treating them as
simultaneous. /cart latency stepped first, by a margin large enough to be unambiguous at
five-minute resolution and not attributable to alerting delay. /orders did not move until after
that step.

That single observation inverted the assumed direction. If /cart moved first, /orders was not the
origin, and an explanation was required that started on the read path. The cache hit rate
correlation found shortly afterwards supplied one, and the escalation to fulfilment-core carried
both facts rather than a general report of degradation.

The general point, which is why this document exists: **when two endpoints on one backend degrade
together, which of them moved first is evidence, and the louder endpoint is not reliably the
earlier one.** Corroboration establishes that a shared cause is likely. It does not establish where
the shared cause sits, and the instinct to start from the endpoint generating the most noise
actively works against finding out.

## Mechanism

Established by fulfilment-core, not from the gateway view, and summarised here at the level the
gateway can corroborate.

Both endpoints consult the same cached inventory state — /cart to display a basket, /orders to
validate against before accepting a submission. Several days before the incident a routine bulk
refresh rewrote a large population of those entries within a short span, and every entry written in
that operation received the same lifetime. They therefore expired together, on a timer, days after
the operation that created them.

At expiry, a large share of requests on both endpoints stopped being served from cache and began
performing full lookups. That is slower per request, and the effect appeared as a step rather than
a ramp, which is what a synchronised expiry looks like and is not what organic demand looks like.

Errors did not appear at the same moment. A full lookup was slower but initially still returned
inside the callers' timeouts. Only as the miss rate stayed high did an increasing share of requests
cross the deadline and be returned as failures. The interval between the latency step and the
first errors is the headroom between slow-path response time and the timeout, which is the same
relationship RB-PAY-000 describes as a general principle. This incident is a straightforward
instance of it on a different service, arising without any change having been deployed.

## Resolution and what was changed

Fulfilment-core repopulated the affected cached state ahead of demand. Both endpoints returned
within objective over the following minutes.

Recovery following repopulation is consistent with the mechanism above. It is not what established
it. The mechanism was established from the expiry timing and from the uniform lifetimes recorded
against the earlier refresh, both of which were verifiable independently of the recovery. This
distinction is laboured here deliberately: had the team reasoned backwards from the recovery, the
same recovery would have been equally consistent with several explanations that were not
investigated, and the durable fix would have been chosen against the wrong one.

Two changes were made. Cached inventory entries now receive lifetimes distributed across a range
rather than a single value, so a bulk write cannot produce a synchronised expiry. Bulk refresh
operations now record when they ran and against how many entries, so that a correlation of this
kind is available to a responder rather than requiring the owning team to reconstruct it.

## Lessons

1. Not every incident has a change to correlate against. This one had no deployment within twelve
   hours in any direction, and the cause had been set in motion days earlier by an operation
   nobody would describe as a change.
2. Corroboration between two endpoints on one backend is a strong signal about *where* to look and
   says nothing about *what* is wrong. It was read correctly at T+4 and the incident still went
   sideways for twenty-three minutes afterwards.
3. Onset ordering between corroborating endpoints is evidence and is cheap to check. It should be
   checked before a hypothesis is formed rather than after one has failed.
4. The endpoint producing the most complaints is not necessarily the endpoint the problem started
   on, and the correlation between volume of complaint and proximity to cause is weak.
5. A write path being the more dangerous endpoint is a reason to be careful with it, not a reason
   to believe it is at fault.
6. An outcome of EVIDENCE INSUFFICIENT summarised as "not elevated" removed a degraded endpoint
   from the incident for several minutes. The distinction between the two is not pedantry and it
   had a measurable cost here.
7. Where the two series disagree on an endpoint, both belong in the summary. A single-word status
   for an endpoint that is late but not yet failing loses the information that matters most early
   in an incident.
