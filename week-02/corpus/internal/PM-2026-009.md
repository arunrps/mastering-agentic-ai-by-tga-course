# Capacity Saturation During a Traffic Surge

Incident date 2026-03-02. Severity 3. Duration 48 minutes from first alert to recovery. Owning
team catalog-discovery. Times are given as offsets from the first alert.

## Summary

/search and /products degraded on latency during a period of substantially elevated request
volume. The 5xx rate on both endpoints remained near baseline throughout and at no point cleared
the evidence gate. No deployment was involved. The condition resolved as volume receded, without
intervention, and no change was made to any service during the incident.

The incident is recorded not because the mechanism was interesting — it was not — but because
twenty-six of the forty-eight minutes were spent investigating a deployment that had nothing to do
with it.

## Impact

Product search and product detail were slower than objective for the duration. Requests completed.
Nothing failed at a rate distinguishable from an ordinary day. Affected-request counts were high in
absolute terms, as they always are on these endpoints, but the severity assessment under
POL-SEV-001 turned on the rate condition rather than the count, and the rate never reached a
threshold above Sev 3.

## Timeline

**T+0.** Latency alert on /search, p95 above objective.

**T+3.** /products confirmed elevated on latency. Error rates on both endpoints checked and found
near baseline.

**T+6.** Change correlation attempted. A deployment to an unrelated service was found within the
preceding hour.

**T+6 to T+32.** The investigation pursued that deployment. See below.

**T+32.** Request volume examined. Volume was found to be substantially above the comparable
period on preceding days across both endpoints, and the latency profile was found to track it.

**T+35.** Capacity assessed as the working explanation. Catalog-discovery informed. No action
taken.

**T+48.** Volume receded; latency returned within objective without intervention. Incident closed.

## The deployment that was not involved

At T+6 a responder searched for recent changes, found a deployment to a service other than
catalog-service, and adopted it as the working hypothesis. Twenty-six minutes went into
establishing a connection between that deployment and the observed latency. No connection was
found, because there was not one.

The reasoning at the time was not unreasonable in isolation. A change had occurred, it was recent,
and it was the only recent change anyone could point at. The problem is that those three facts are
true on most days, at most hours, on a platform of this size. A deployment being the most recent
notable event is a property of deployment frequency, not evidence about the incident.

What made it costly was the order of operations. The change search ran before the volume check
rather than after it. Had volume been examined first, it would have supplied an explanation in
under a minute and the deployment would never have been a candidate. Instead a candidate was
adopted early, and everything observed afterwards was assessed for consistency with it. Evidence
that did not fit was set aside as noise rather than treated as disconfirming, and the volume figure
that eventually resolved the incident had been available in the same view from T+0.

Two further observations. The deployment in question was subsequently and correctly validated by
its own owning team under the ordinary validation process, and no rollback was requested or
performed. And the twenty-six minutes were not spent idly: the responder was working, thoroughly,
on the wrong question, which is harder to notice from inside an incident than being stuck is.

## Mechanism

Request volume across both catalog endpoints rose substantially above the comparable period. Both
endpoints slowed. Neither failed at a rate above baseline.

This is what capacity looks like and it has a signature that distinguishes it from a fault. Latency
scaled with volume — it rose as volume rose and receded as volume receded, with no independent
onset of its own. Both endpoints on the backend moved together, which in this case reflected common
cause rather than a shared fault: both were subject to the same rise in demand. Errors stayed near
baseline throughout, because nothing was broken and requests were being served, only late.

That last property is the useful one. A fault that degrades a backend generally produces some
failure signal alongside the latency, and a rise in latency with errors flat and volume elevated is
a substantially different situation from a rise in latency with errors flat and volume unchanged.
The second case requires an explanation. The first, in most cases, already has one.

## Resolution

None. The condition resolved as volume receded. No change was deployed, none was rolled back, and
no configuration was altered during the incident.

Recording "no action taken" as a resolution is uncomfortable and there was pressure during the
incident to do something visible. It is nonetheless the correct record. An intervention made at
T+40 would have been followed by recovery at T+48 and would have been credited with it, and the
platform would now hold a belief about a remedy that had no effect.

## What was changed afterwards

The runbook ordering was revised so that the capacity check runs before change correlation on
catalog-service endpoints, which is the ordering RB-CAT-000 now carries. Capacity headroom was
reviewed separately by catalog-discovery outside the incident process, and any changes arising from
that review are recorded elsewhere and are not part of this incident.

## Lessons

1. Not every incident has a change to correlate against. A deployment existing within the window
   is not evidence of involvement, and on a platform deploying at ordinary frequency there is
   almost always one to find.
2. Check volume before searching for changes when latency is elevated and errors are not. The
   volume check is faster, and where it explains the observation it removes the need for the change
   search entirely.
3. A hypothesis adopted early is expensive to dislodge, and the cost is not in the time spent
   testing it but in the evidence that gets discounted while it stands. The volume figure was
   visible from the first minute.
4. Elevated latency with errors at baseline is a distinct condition and warrants a distinct set of
   checks. It is not a milder version of a failure incident.
5. Where a condition resolves without intervention, record that. An intervention made shortly
   before an unrelated recovery will be credited with it, and the belief is difficult to correct
   afterwards.
